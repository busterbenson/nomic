# 10. Social dynamics: alliances, kingmaking, betrayal, griefing

Researched 2026-11-20. Sourced material on voting blocs and kingmaking in Nomic is thin; what follows is mostly early Agora and BlogNomic.

## Summary
- Early Agora survived two near-fatal bad-faith moves; what saved it was players accepting a majority's decision about the game itself.
- Agora now writes its conduct rules down: a ban switch, a way back, and a formal goodbye.
- Sock puppets and inattentive voting did more damage than open betrayal.

## Sources
- **Agora's first year** ([Vlad, 1999](https://agoranomic.org/Herald/theses/html/1999-XX-XX-Vlad.html)): the Walrus Scam (1994) got round the ban on paying voters with a currency that barely existed; a judgment that the walruses never existed made its author quit, and he got the title Scamster. The Black Repeals (April 1994) proposed repealing every rule to collect a reward and passed because many players assumed they'd fail; CFJ 143 stopped them and the rule was fixed. Early activity sat with a few prolific proposers.
- **Agora's conduct rules** ([ruleset](https://agoranomic.org/ruleset/slr.txt)): Rule 2679, "Banned is a secured negative boolean person switch"; an unwelcome person "CANNOT register or be registered"; harmful actions "may include" personal attacks and "provocative/bad-faith comments"; an unwelcome person may ask for forgiveness once a quarter. Rule 869: "The rules CANNOT compel players to unduly harass non-players." Rule 1789, Cantus Cygneus: a leaving player can publish a statement of grievances.
- **BlogNomic's Mad Emperor** ([round 6](https://kevan.org/gndt/bnhistory/round6.html)): a second account, "Paul", "appeared to strengthen Anthony's veto power"; a Call for Judgement removed the Emperor and both accounts left.
- **Conspiracies** ([ais523, 2010](https://agoranomic.org/Herald/theses/html/2010-08-10-ais523.html)): an "automated cronjob acting on behalf of ais523" lost because a rival conspiracy had already used the same loophole.

## Patterns that worked
- Settle disputes with a judgment procedure, not an argument.
- Give a scam status (a title) rather than only punishing it.
- Make leaving explicit and dignified, and coming back possible.
- Fix the loophole in the rules after a scam, not just reverse the scam.

## Problems to avoid
- One person, several seats (Paul doubled a veto).
- Free rewards for submitting things invite spam.
- Votes cast on the assumption something will fail.
- Activity concentrated in a few players.

## What it means for Promptocracy
- With 8 players and "last one alive" as the win, every alliance is temporary and betrayal is how you win. It should be visible and fun, and a line between a move and harassment belongs in the rules; Agora's Rule 2679 is the nearest model. (Library note.)
- The Champion's change passes unless every other player votes against it, so one ally is enough to let it through. Alliances with the Champion are the strongest position in the game. (Library note.)
- Not voting counts as no for ordinary proposals, which already protects against the Black Repeals kind of inattention. See [voting](03-voting.md) and [loopholes and scams](05-loopholes-and-scams.md).
- Rule 204 lets a player take their own turns through an AI client. Nothing yet says one person can't hold two seats; Paul's case says it should. (Library note.)
