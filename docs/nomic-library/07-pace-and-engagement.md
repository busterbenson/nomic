# 7. Pace and engagement: cadence, idle players, onboarding

Researched 2026-11-20.

## Summary
- BlogNomic runs asynchronously, idles players automatically after seven days, runs dynasties of about a month, and is always open to new players.
- Agora is 33 years old and has around 15-20 active players out of about 200 who have come and gone.
- Idle rules are necessary and also a source of grievance; heavy ones shape how people burn out.

## Sources
- **BlogNomic** ([FAQ](https://wiki.blognomic.com/index.php?title=FAQ)): "Players idle out automatically if they don't do anything for seven whole days." Players can ask to be idled and "come back at some point in the future by asking to be unidled." A dynasty is a round: "Each round lasts about a month, on average." Then "the ruleset gets cut back to the very basic proposal-making rules," and "the winner gets to pick a theme." "The game is always open to new players"; each new player gets a Mentor.
- **Weaponized idling** ([failed proposal](https://blognomic.com/archive/less_weaponized_idling_core)): near a dynasty's end "the four-day idling rule suddenly becomes much more powerful"; admins can't see private actions, so active players can look idle; and players who act but never vote can "grind stuff to a halt."
- **Burnout** ([BlogNomic, 2011](https://blognomic.com/archive/burnout)): "I reallly, really want to go idle for a week or so. The current rules penalize me for doing so too harshly, though, so I can't." The advice in reply: "stay minimally active and just post a random vote."
- **Agora** ([front page](https://agoranomic.org/)): "At 33 years old, Agora is the longest running nomic. It has also seen around 200 players come and go." Rule 869: "A player, acting as emself, CAN deregister (cease being a player) by announcement." "The game may be won, but the game never ends."
- **Who is even playing?** ([Vanyel's thesis](https://agoranomic.org/Herald/theses/html/XXXX-XX-XX-Vanyel.html)) describes a player "thought to have been deregistered for months" who wasn't, and argues for continuing as reported rather than recalculating months of quorums.

## Patterns that worked
- Automatic idling with a quorum that adjusts, and a cheap way back.
- Voluntary idle as a break that isn't quitting.
- Rounds that cut the ruleset back (BlogNomic dynasties): bloat stays down and newcomers get a fresh start.
- Join any time, with a mentor.

## Problems to avoid
- Idle penalties too heavy to take a short break.
- Idle timers that can't see what players actually do.
- End-of-round speed-ups that idle active players by accident.
- Not knowing who is currently a player, which breaks every count.
- A game that never ends keeps only a small core.

## What it means for Promptocracy
- Promptocracy has no idle rule yet, and not voting counts as no. On a daily game with 8 players, one absent living player blocks every unanimous change, and now that more rules will be unanimous, that matters more. A short, visible idle rule (a few missed days) with a one-tap way back is worth considering. (Library note, not decided.)
- The Champion's veto needs every other player to vote against, and not voting doesn't count, so idle players already can't block a Champion change.
- Eras already give Promptocracy BlogNomic's round reset. A lighter ruleset at each Era, with the Champion naming what carries over, would keep the book readable. (Library note.)
- Invites mid-game (102.3) are BlogNomic's "always open". A generated short ruleset for a new player's first day is the mentor's job done by the moderator. (Library note.)
