# 9. Eras: dynasties, rounds and resets

Researched 2026-11-20.

## Summary
- BlogNomic splits play into **dynasties**: a winner becomes Emperor, runs the next dynasty under a new theme, and the game waits in an "Interregnum" until the new Emperor posts an Ascension Address.
- Early dynasties usually ended with a player exploiting the dynasty's own win condition, so the reset came out of play, not a clock.
- Agora has no resets, only **Rounds** marked by wins: "The game may be won, but the game never ends."

## Sources
- **Agora** ([ruleset](https://agoranomic.org/ruleset/flr.txt)): Rule 101, "The game may be won, but the game never ends." Rule 1023: a Round "is the period of time between a pivot and the next pivot", a pivot being "an instant at which at least one person won the game."
- **BlogNomic's first year** (Kevan's history: [round 2](https://kevan.org/gndt/bnhistory/round2.html), [round 5](https://kevan.org/gndt/bnhistory/round5.html), [round 6](https://kevan.org/gndt/bnhistory/round6.html)):
  - First Dynasty of Myke (Apr-May 2003): "It is my hope that this First Dynasty of Myke will be one full of cooperation." It ended when the Grand Vizier out-earned the Emperor under Myke's own Imperial Bankruptcy win condition and "overthrew the government." Admins earned gold faster than other players, which caused a row.
  - First Dynasty of Kevan (Orwellian): ended when Anthony became Emperor through the Labour Union win condition. A citizen override of the Imperial Veto (Turnover) carried into the next dynasty.
  - First Dynasty of Anthony ("Mad Emperor", Sept 2003): four days long. Anthony vetoed the proposal to remove him, and a Call for Judgement removed him instead. "No proposals passed during the dynasty, and eight proposals were illegal."
  - Themes over the years: Kingdom, Robin Hood, Agricultural, Orwellian, Robot Factory, Galactic, Superhero, Odyssey, Crashed Spaceship, Office, Petri-Dish.
- **Today's BlogNomic** ([ais523](https://blognomic.com/archive/moving_forwards)): "If the game is in an Interregnum then the new Concierge must make an Ascension Address." Players who rank themselves by dynasties held create "an incentive to start a dynasty and abandon it quickly."

## Patterns that worked
- A theme changes the game's vocabulary and puzzle in one step, and gives new players a fair way in.
- The end is a win condition the dynasty's own players wrote, so the last move is also the best story.
- A small set of core rules survives every reset and keeps the meta-game stable.
- The Emperor's power is checked: a citizen override, and a judgment that can remove a bad Emperor.

## Problems to avoid
- An Emperor with an unchecked veto can freeze a whole dynasty (Anthony's passed nothing).
- Rulers who earn faster than players skew a points race (Myke's).
- If holding the title is the status, the cheapest play is to win and abandon the Era.
- Interregnum rules need exact wording; "address posted" vs "reset performed" caused real disputes.
- No source described what makes a reset feel fresh rather than like losing progress.

## What it means for Promptocracy
- Decided: an Era's winner becomes Champion, names the next Era, and can change one rule without a vote; it passes unless every other player votes against it, and not voting doesn't count as against. The ultimate win is being the only player alive.
- That makes the Champion close to BlogNomic's Emperor, with a weaker check: one friend of the Champion, or one absent player, lets the change through. A Champion who can touch the veto, the win condition or the marks could lock the game the way the Mad Emperor did. (Library note.)
- A limit on holding the Championship in consecutive Eras answers the "win and abandon" incentive. (Library note.)
- Let each Era's players write the next win condition during the Era, as BlogNomic's dynasties did. (Library note.)
