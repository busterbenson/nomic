# Rules voice

Buster, 2026-11-19: "crisp board-game rulebook", and "use the player's own words whenever it can be done in a clear way".

1. Talk to the player: "you", present tense. "You can move one tile a day."
2. One idea per sentence. Say what you can do first, then the limit: "You can withdraw your proposal until midnight, unless you've ended your turn."
3. Everyday words. "can", not "may" or "shall". No legalese: hereby, pursuant to, in the event that, aforementioned, notwithstanding.
4. Keep the game's own terms, the same every time: moderator, proposal, draft, ballot, block, mark, clause, turn, day, tile, piece.
5. Digits for numbers (3, not three). Cite rules by number in parentheses: (202.4).
6. A player's own words stay when they are clear, fit the rule's terms and say exactly what they mean. Reword only to make it exact, to match a game term, or to fix the grammar.
7. Nothing in a rule that isn't a rule: no flavor text, no asides, no "note that". The fun is in what the rules let you do.

Before: "The proposer may withdraw a confirmed proposal until it goes on the ballot, unless they have ended their turn (rule 202.4). A withdrawn proposal doesn't use up the day's proposal."
After: "You can withdraw your proposal until midnight, unless you've ended your turn (202.4). Withdrawing frees up your proposal for the day."
